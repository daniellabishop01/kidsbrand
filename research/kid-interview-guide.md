# Kid Interview Guide: Building the Brand With Kids

*For conversations with kids ages 6–12 about their world, their wins, and what makes a brand feel like it's "for them". Use the answers to shape the name, voice, mascot, flavors, packaging and the Brag Board.*

---

## Before you start

**Ground rules (protect the kids, protect the brand)**
- **Get written parent consent** before every session. A parent or guardian should be present or nearby.
- **Keep it short:** about 20 minutes for ages 6–8, 30 minutes for ages 9–12. Stop when they get wiggly.
- **Don't ask about diagnoses, ADHD, medications or health conditions.** Talk about everyday moments (homework, feelings, sleep), never labels. If a kid brings something up, listen kindly and move on.
- **Online or recorded sessions:** collecting information from kids under 13 is covered by COPPA (the federal children's privacy law). Get parent consent for any recording, and don't keep names, photos or voices longer than you need them.
- **Thank them:** a small thank-you gift (stickers, a book) for the kid and a note to the parent.

**How to get honest answers from kids**
- **Kids want to please grown-ups.** Avoid yes/no questions like "Do you like this?" (they'll say yes). Ask "What would you change?" or "Some kids say ___, others say ___. What do you think?"
- **Show, don't just ask.** Use pictures, cards, drawings and sticker voting. Kids say more with their hands than with words.
- **Ask "why?" gently, and only once.** Asking repeatedly feels like a test.
- **Watch faces, not just answers.** A grin, an eye-roll or a "huh?" tells you more than "it's good".
- **Talk to kids in pairs or small groups of 3–4 friends** for the fun sections. One-on-one is better for the feelings sections.
- **Split by age.** 6–8-year-olds and 9–12-year-olds will react very differently, especially to anything that might feel "babyish".

**Bring:** printed name cards, printed voice lines, mascot sketches or animal pictures, packaging mockups (even rough ones), sticker sheets for voting, paper and markers, and flavor samples if you have them (with parent permission and allergy check).

---

## 1. Warm-up: getting to know them *(3 minutes)*

1. What's your name, and how old are you?
2. If you could have any superpower for one day, what would it be?
3. What's the best thing that happened to you this week?
4. What are you really, really good at? (If they're shy: "What would your best friend say you're good at?")

---

## 2. Their world: what feels "for kids like me" *(5 minutes)*

*Helps us understand their taste, so the brand doesn't feel too young or too old.*

5. What's your favorite show, movie, game or book right now? What do you love about it?
6. Who's your favorite character ever? What makes them so great?
7. What's something that's "too babyish" for kids your age? What's something that feels "grown-up cool"?
8. What's a brand or thing you own that makes you feel proud to have it? Why?
9. Who's a grown-up (not your mom or dad) you love spending time with? What do they say or do that makes you feel awesome?
   *(This tests our "cool uncle, aunt or grandpa" voice. Listen for the exact words kids use.)*

---

## 3. Wins and celebrating *(5 minutes)*

*Helps shape "Small wins. Big deal." and the Brag Board.*

10. What's something you did that made you feel really proud?
11. When you do something great, how do you like people to celebrate? (A high five? A hug? Telling everyone? A prize? Something quiet?)
12. What's a "small win"? Can you think of one from today?
13. Do you like sticker charts or reward charts? What makes one fun, and what makes one boring?
14. If you earned a special badge for something, what would you want it to be for? What would it be called?
15. Some kids like to show off their wins, and some like to keep them private. Which are you?

---

## 4. Tricky moments and feeling supported *(5 minutes, one-on-one)*

*Helps us talk about hard moments the way kids do, without labels. Keep it light and kind.*

16. What part of the day is the hardest for you? What makes it tricky?
17. What's homework time like at your house? What would make it better?
18. When you have really big feelings, what helps you feel better?
19. When something is hard, what do you wish a grown-up would *say* to you? What do you wish they'd *stop* saying?
20. Do you like doing hard things on your own, or with someone next to you? Why?
    *(This tests "Let's do this. Together.")*
21. What helps you fall asleep at night?

---

## 5. Mornings, vitamins and taste *(4 minutes)*

*Helps with the routine, format and flavors.*

22. What does your morning look like, from waking up to leaving for school?
23. Have you ever taken a vitamin? What was it like? What did you like or not like?
24. Would you rather chew something, drink something, or eat something with your breakfast? Why?
25. If you mixed a powder into water, what color would you want it to turn? Would that be cool or weird?
26. **Flavor game:** put a sticker on your top 3 flavors (show cards: berry, grape, watermelon, lemonade, orange, tropical, cherry, mango, "mystery flavor", and others).
27. Make up a name for a flavor you'd want to try.

---

## 6. Names: what sounds fun *(5 minutes)*

*Read each name card out loud, then have kids say it too.*

28. Which name is the most fun to say? Say it like you're cheering!
29. Which name sounds like it's for kids **your age**? Which sounds like it's for **little kids**? Which sounds like it's for **grown-ups**?
30. If you heard this name, what do you think it would be? (A toy? A snack? A game? A vitamin?)
31. Which name would you be proud to have in your house? Which would you be embarrassed by?
32. **Sticker vote:** put one sticker on your favorite name and one on the name you like least.

*Tip: also include 1–2 "decoy" names that are clearly babyish. If kids pick those, they may be telling you what they think you want to hear.*

---

## 7. Voice test: does it sound like the cool uncle? *(4 minutes)*

*Read each line out loud. For each one, kids hold up a card: 😊 "I like it", 😐 "It's okay", 🙄 "Cringe" (or "Too babyish").*

| Line to test |
|---|
| "Small wins. Big deal." |
| "Let's do this. Together." |
| "Homework finished? I'd expect nothing less." |
| "Seven days in a row. I'm telling everyone." |
| "Big feelings are just big hearts in disguise." |
| "Today's forecast: 100% chance of you being brilliant." |
| "Brilliance, now in stick form." |
| "You and me? Unstoppable." |
| "Today has no idea what's coming." |

33. Which line would you want to see on your box every morning?
34. Which line sounds like something a cool grown-up would actually say?
35. Which one would make you roll your eyes? Why?
36. If you could write a line for the box, what would it say?

---

## 8. Mascot: who's in their corner? *(4 minutes)*

37. If this brand had a character, what would it be? An animal? A creature? A person? Something totally made up?
38. **Draw it!** What's it called? What does it say when you do something great?
39. Is your character funny, wise, brave, silly, calm, or something else?
40. Show the sketches or animal cards: which character would you want cheering you on? Which one looks too babyish?
41. If the character could do one thing with you every day, what would it be?

---

## 9. The box: making it feel special *(3 minutes)*

*Show a rough mockup if you have one.*

42. What would make opening this box feel exciting?
43. What would you want to find inside? (Stickers? A chart? A note? A surprise?)
44. What would you do with the stickers?
45. What would make you want to keep the box instead of throwing it away?
46. What would make a kid your age want to tell a friend about it?

---

## 10. Wrap-up *(2 minutes)*

47. If you could invent anything to help kids have better days, what would it be?
48. What's one thing grown-ups always get wrong about kids your age?
49. What should we make sure we **never** do?
50. If you could tell this brand one thing, what would it be?

---

## Quick version (10 minutes, for busy settings like sports fields or school events)

1. What's the best thing that happened to you this week?
2. What's "too babyish" for kids your age?
3. When you do something great, how do you like to celebrate?
4. What do you wish grown-ups would say when something is hard?
5. Do you like doing hard things alone or with someone next to you?
6. **Name vote:** which is most fun to say? Which sounds too babyish?
7. **Line vote:** 😊 / 😐 / 🙄 on 3–4 voice lines.
8. Draw or describe a character who'd cheer you on.

---

## Add-on: 5 quick questions for the parent *(while kids draw)*

1. What made you say yes to this conversation? What are you hoping for your child?
2. When your child has a great day, what does it look like?
3. Whose words does your child light up around? What do they say?
4. What would make you trust a supplement brand for your child? What would make you walk away?
5. Which names and lines did you like? Which made you wince?

---

## After each session: capture it while it's fresh

| What to write down | Example |
|---|---|
| **Exact words kids used** (gold for copywriting) | "My grandpa says I'm 'a tough cookie'." |
| **Favorite name and least favorite name**, with the reason | "Huzzah: sounds like a party!" |
| **Lines that got 😊 vs 🙄** | "Today's forecast…" got 4 smiles and 1 eye-roll |
| **Mascot ideas and drawings** | Photograph every drawing |
| **Celebration preferences** | "Likes quiet wins, hates being put on the spot" |
| **Surprises** | Anything you didn't expect |
| **Age group** | 6–8 or 9–12 |

**Aim for:** at least **12–20 kids**, split across ages 6–8 and 9–12, boys and girls, and a mix of kids who find focus easy and kids who find it hard (as described by parents, never labeled). Look for patterns that show up again and again, not one standout answer.
